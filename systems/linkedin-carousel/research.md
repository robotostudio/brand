# Best-performing LinkedIn carousels: Roboto Studio niche

**Prepared:** 21 Oct 2026, for Roboto Studio (a Sanity / Next.js / headless CMS web agency).
**Purpose:** reference for planning and designing Roboto's LinkedIn carousels (document/PDF posts).
**Evidence rules:** engagement figures are as shown on each public post page on 21 Oct 2026. Follower counts are the author's. Anything not confirmed is marked TBC. Do not invent or round figures.

## Summary

- Carousels in this niche get little engagement overall. Of 10 examples, only 3 got more than 100 reactions.
- The top performers show real work: an actual workflow, or a client site rebuild. Advice decks did worse.
- Pure headless CMS comparison carousels did worst. The best pure Sanity/headless example got 32 reactions.
- High-performing carousels are rare in this niche, so there's an opening. But there's no strong benchmark to aim for.

## Performance data (2025–26)

- **Slide count:** 9–12 slides is the consensus. Avoid 5 or fewer.
  - **MagicPost** (67,765 carousels): median likes were 22 at 2–5 slides, 26 at 6–8, 49 at 9–12, 58 at 13–20 and 60 at 21+. They note that more committed authors make longer decks, so effort explains some of this. https://magicpost.in/blog/how-many-slides-in-a-linkedin-carousel
  - **Ligo Social:** 11–15 pages +15.6%, 16+ pages +25.7%, 1–10 pages slightly negative. https://ligosocial.com/research/data/linkedin-2026-documents-video.csv
  - **SEO Works** (only 76 posts): 7–9 slides did best. https://www.seoworks.co.uk/downloads/data-led-analysis-of-linkedin-video-vs-carousels/
- **Format:**
  - **Metricool** (673,658 posts): carousels get about 11× the interactions of image posts, partly because swipes count as clicks. https://metricool.com/wp-content/uploads/Linkedin-Study-2026-EN.pdf
  - **Creator:** 4.8–6.2% engagement, with 4:5 at 1080×1350 recommended. Its methodology is thin, so treat this as directional only. https://www.getcreator.io/stats/linkedin-carousel-benchmarks
- **Cover and text density:** these are recommendations, not measured data. Covers of 6–10 words with a number, a pain point or an outcome. Body slides of about 18–30 words, one idea each. Sources: https://grouglobal.com/blog/linkedin-carousel-strategy and https://postunreel.com/blog/linkedin-carousel-hook-formulas-stop-the-scroll

## Patterns

### Slide copy
- One idea per slide.
- Short headlines, with the key word in the accent colour.
- A repeating format: numbered mistakes or rules, bad vs good, or X vs Y.
- End on a summary or cheat-sheet slide, then a question.
- The top post used 2–4 words a slide. Paragraph-heavy decks did worse.

### Visual style
- One fixed template with logo, slide counter and "Swipe →" on every slide.
- Two or three colours.
- 4:5 portrait slides. The top post was square; landscape underperformed.
- Real product and site screenshots or mockups beat generic illustration for credibility. The top two posts both used them.

### Post copy (caption)
- Line 1 is a bold claim or a credibility line ("After 20+ audits…", "Most X make the same mistakes").
- Line 2 is a short reframe.
- Then 60–250 words that sum up the deck as a list (arrows, numbers or ✅).
- Most close with a question or "Save/Share this".
- Hashtags are optional. The top post had none.

## Recommendations for Roboto Studio

1. **Lead with real work, not advice.** The top two posts were "here's my actual workflow" and "here's a client site we rebuilt". Roboto candidates: the Paper + Claude design workflow ("3 weeks to about 20 seconds"; this claim still needs Clark to confirm it), or a real Sanity rebuild shown with real Sanity Studio and live-site screenshots.
2. **Use a dark charcoal look in Jono's voice.** Follow the Ciccone/Kulkarni style: charcoal background, white type, one accent colour (not yellow), 2–6 words a slide, 9–12 slides, 4:5 portrait. Possible cover lines: "Your website doesn't need the best CMS." or "Bad redesigns vs good redesigns."
3. **Aim at buyer pain, not CMS jargon.** Headless comparison decks did poorly. Redesign risk did better: traffic loss, the site breaking when it's handed to the content team, AI sites that all look the same.
4. **Caption in Jono's voice.** Blunt first line in first person, a short list, one question to close, light punctuation, no hashtags.
